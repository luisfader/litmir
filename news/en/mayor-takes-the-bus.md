SINEA. — On his first morning as Mayor, Charles did not go to City Hall by car. He took the bus. The bus was new, it was clean, and it was free.

"Mama, is that the Mayor? On the bus?" a small girl asked. Her mother did not answer. Our reporter, who was sitting behind them, can confirm: yes, it was.

## A promise is a promise

Free buses were the first promise of Charles's campaign, together with two more: to fight the mafia and corruption, and to respect all immigrants. "In Sinea, no one is illegal," he said in his famous speech in the main square. One year ago, nobody believed a man with no papers and a small bag could win. He won. Then he lost. Then he won again.

This time there were no hackers and no false papers. Charles is now a legal resident of Sinea, with real papers. "Not made on a computer," as the judge said.

## The cleanest debate in history

Charles's rival in the election was a young doctor. Political experts were shocked by their TV debate: nobody said anything bad about immigrants. They talked about schools, hospitals and buses. Some viewers called the station to complain that it was boring. Others called to ask if it could happen again.

The result was close. Very close. At the end of the night, the doctor shook Charles's hand: "Congratulations, Mr. Mayor. Do your best." Charles answered with a job offer. He wants to open a free hospital for workers, and he needs good people.

## Welcome to Sinea

At the port, a ship arrived yesterday morning. A young man stood alone next to the water with a small bag in his hand. He did not know where to go. Two police officers, a man and a woman, walked to him.

One year ago, this was the start of a bribe. Yesterday, the officers said: "Welcome to Sinea. Are you lost? Do you need help?" They sent the young man to the new **Welcome House** on the main square, where people can get help with papers, a bed and a job. For free. "And you can take the bus," the policewoman said. "The bus is free too."

The Mayor, who was there by chance, offered to show the young man the way.

*The Sinea Herald* is also free. Like the buses.
