FOUR YEARS AFTER THE DEVASTATION. — The Clear Path, the movement that controls several sectors of the ruined city, has once again explained its philosophy to the public. As usual, the explanation was given by two men, one with a shotgun and one with a chainsaw.

The principles of the movement are simple. Every man owns himself. Every man's will and property are his only law. "The old systems were a cage," the man with the shotgun told a new arrival in the street. "We burned the cage! Here, your safety is your business. Your justice is your business."

No appeals. No handouts. You get what you are strong enough to hold onto. According to the movement, this is "the purest form of respect". In this sector, people show respect by acknowledging the Crew.

## A debate in a shop

Last week, the philosophy was tested in a small market with empty shelves. The Clear Path wanted to take a drifter away for forced labour. The reason: he had entered their sector and asked for food.

A stranger who introduced himself as "just Robert" asked a few questions.

Did the drifter borrow anything? No. Did he steal anything? No. Did he damage anything? No. Did they give him food? Also no. "So why are you trying to take him?" Robert asked. Witnesses describe the silence that followed as "beautiful".

The Clear Path then offered a market solution: the drifter had a debt, and Robert could buy him. Robert opened his bag and showed three packets of antibiotics. The two sides negotiated. The price went up to three packets.

Then Robert closed the bag. "I'm not buying a person," he said. "I wanted to see whether you actually believed your own rules."

The rest of the debate was conducted in the dark, after somebody switched off the lights. The drifter left through the back door. The Clear Path did not issue a statement, but several shots were fired.

## The opposition has a job offer

The drifter, Vasily, now rides an old single-cylinder motorcycle. He has a list of three names: a surgeon at the railway hospital near North Station, a water engineer at Raven Dam and an agronomist who protects a seed collection at Pinefield. The settlement he is working for is called **Our Utopia**.

Its recruitment policy is unusual for these times. It offers food, shelter, work and a say in what it is building. It does not promise money it doesn't have or safety it can't guarantee. And, according to its founder, it definitely does not recruit people the way the Clear Path does.

*The Sinea Herald* notes that this is the first job offer in four years that does not include a shotgun.
