A local lawyer, Rosa, has a proposal to make dating safer: first dates should follow the rules of a court.

Rosa explained her method during a first date last week, in a small coffee shop. She wore a black suit. Her date, a man who introduced himself as "Mark", did not know he was taking part in a legal experiment.

"Tell me about your life," Rosa told him. "I will analyse it. Like in a court."

## Exhibit A, Exhibit B

According to witnesses, the date followed a clear procedure. First, Rosa asked about the man's intentions. Was this a friendly meeting or a long-term relationship? "I need to know the rules," she said. His answer, "I want to know you," was rejected. It was too general. "The court does not like general answers."

Then Rosa opened a small notebook and presented the evidence:

- **Exhibit A:** he was nervous, which is not good for a first date.
- **Exhibit B:** he did not bring flowers, which shows a lack of romantic intention.

When "Mark" asked her a question of his own — where was she two years ago? — Rosa objected loudly. The question, she ruled, was not relevant. She also wanted to know if he had evidence against her.

## Why the question?

*The Sinea Herald* can reveal that "Mark" is not his real name. He is Frank Stevenson, a private detective. Two years ago, his brother Tom went on an online date and never came back. The police called it an accident. Frank believes a woman killed him, so he created a fake profile and started dating.

His list of dates so far includes a woman who asks too many questions, a woman who talks to the moon, and now a lawyer. Our sources say the next one plays the violin.

## The verdict

Frank finished his coffee fast and left. "The court is not happy with this," Rosa said.

Asked for a comment on the new dating law, the detective said only that it was not a date: it was a trial.

*The Sinea Herald* reminds readers that in Sinea any new law, including a dating law, must first be approved by the Mayor. The Mayor is already engaged.
