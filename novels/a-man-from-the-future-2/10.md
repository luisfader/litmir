### Luzhniki Stadium · July 6, 2025

On the morning of departure day, the machines stood in long rows on the football field. There were almost three thousand of them, red and shining, like a giant box of chocolates. Men in silver suits were climbing inside, saying goodbye to Moscow, and taking photos with everything they could find: trees, dogs, ice cream, a very tired police officer.

The men who passed the course stood on one side of the field. There were two hundred and six of them. They were wearing normal clothes now: jeans, T-shirts, and — in Boris's case — a shirt with flowers on it that Svetlana bought him “as a test”.

Alex walked between the machines with Pavel's recorder in his hand. He was checking that every machine had the correct settings. HOME. 3024. Same day, same time.

Suddenly, the recorder in his hand started beeping.

“A new message,” Alex said. Olga, Nina, and Svetlana came closer. He pressed the button.

Pavel's face appeared. But this time he wasn't tired. He was shocked. He was standing in the middle of a street in 3024, and behind him, something incredible was happening.

There were women in the street.

“Alex,” Pavel said. His voice was shaking. “You won't believe this. They've come back. The women have come back.”

On the stadium field, three thousand men stopped what they were doing. Somebody dropped an ice cream.

“They weren't gone,” Pavel continued. “They've been on the Moon. For twenty-one years. They've built a whole city there. With gardens, and music, and cafés. They left because…” Pavel looked away from the camera, embarrassed. “Because they were tired of us. Of our rules. Of our gray food. Of men who don't listen. They've watched us all this time from the Moon, and they've seen everything. Including Operation Wife.”

Behind Pavel, a tall woman in a blue coat walked up to the camera. She had silver hair and a very calm face.

“Good afternoon, Moscow,” she said. “My name is Irina. I'm the leader of the Moon City. We've seen what our men have done in your time. We're very sorry. We've also seen the course. The whistle. The lessons about flowers. The man who felt small in a good way.”

Boris, on the field, put his hand on his heart.

“We've decided,” said Irina, “that we will come back to Earth. But only on one condition. Every man in 3024 has to finish this course. Every single one. With the same teachers. And the same whistle.”

The stadium went completely silent. Then three thousand heads turned slowly toward Svetlana.

Svetlana looked at the screen. Then at the men. Then at her whistle.

“How many men are there in 3024?” she asked.

“About four million,” said Irina.

Svetlana closed her eyes.

“I'll need a bigger whistle,” she said.

Alex was still staring at the screen. Twenty-one years. All his life he thought the women had simply disappeared. Nobody explained anything. And all that time, his mother had been on the Moon.

“Irina,” he said. “My mother. Her name is Marina. She sang in the kitchen. A song about a river. I've never forgotten it, but I've never remembered the whole song.”

Irina smiled. She stepped aside, and an older woman came forward. She had Alex's eyes.

She didn't say anything. She just sang. A song about a river, from the beginning to the end.

Alex sat down on the grass. Olga sat next to him and took his hand.

“Have you ever cried in front of three thousand men?” she asked quietly.

“No,” said Alex, with tears on his face. “But I've never had a better reason.”

### Present Perfect

Present Perfect (have / has + третья форма глагола) говорит о результате, важном сейчас, или об опыте «когда-либо». They've come back. We've seen everything. Have you ever cried? I've never forgotten it. Поставьте глаголы в Present Perfect.

1. The women _______ (come) back.

2. They _______ (build) a whole city on the Moon.

3. We _______ (see) the course.

4. Alex _______ (never / forget) the song.

5. _______ you ever _______ (cry) in front of three thousand men?

6. Irina _______ (decide) to come back to Earth.
