### Luzhniki Stadium · June 21, 2025

At eight o'clock in the morning, every screen in Moscow showed the same message. It appeared on the metro, in shops, on buses, and on the giant screen on Tverskaya Street:

*ATTENTION, WORKERS OF 3024. ALL MEN MUST GO TO LUZHNIKI STADIUM. REGISTRATION IS OBLIGATORY. LUNCH IS INCLUDED. YOU MUST NOT EAT THE SEATS.*

Gleb read it on Olga's phone and wiped a tear from his eye.

“It's beautiful,” he said. “It's so official.”

By ten o'clock, three thousand men in silver suits were sitting in the stadium in perfect rows. Nobody had to tell them where to sit. They sat in order of their numbers, from 3001 to 6127. Nina and forty volunteers walked between the rows with mountains of pancakes. The noise of three thousand men eating pancakes at the same time was like a small earthquake.

On the big screen, the mayor appeared.

“Good morning, guests from the future,” he said. “Welcome to Moscow. Please listen carefully. You don't have to worry. Nobody is going to hurt you. But you have to understand one thing. In our time, people don't take other people home like shopping. Women are not available for collection.”

There was a long, sad “Ooooh” from three thousand men.

“Today,” the mayor continued, “the people of Moscow have to vote. They must choose one of three options.”

The options appeared on the screen with pictures that Olga drew in the night.

**1. SOCIALIZE** — the men stay, and the city teaches them to live with people. (The picture showed a man in a silver suit giving flowers to a woman. The flowers were not in his mouth.)

**2. ADAPT** — the men stay, but they live in a special area and slowly learn the rules. (The picture showed a man in a silver suit carefully reading a book called *Please and Thank You*.)

**3. SEND BACK** — the men return to 3024 in their machines. (The picture showed a time machine with a small sign: *Goodbye! Don't come back!*)

The vote lasted all day. People voted in the metro, at work, and on their phones. Television channels talked about nothing else. A famous singer recorded a song called “Don't Take My Wife to 3024”. In Nina's café, two old ladies argued for an hour, and then they voted together.

Svetlana went on television. “I don't have to like these men,” she said, “but I must say they are very good at carrying heavy things. One of them carried my sofa to the fifth floor. Then he asked me to marry him. I said no. He carried the sofa back down. I don't know why.”

At nine o'clock in the evening, the results appeared on the screen.

**SOCIALIZE: 14%**
**ADAPT: 22%**
**SEND BACK: 64%**

The stadium was silent. Three thousand men looked at the screen. Boris's lower lip started to shake. Fedya hid his face in a pancake.

“That's it,” said Gleb quietly. “We're going home.”

But then something happened that nobody expected. Olga's phone started ringing. Then Nina's. Then Svetlana's. Messages were coming in from all over the city — hundreds of them, from women.

*I voted “send back”, but… maybe they just need a teacher?*

*My husband was like that in 1998. Now he cooks. There is hope.*

*Please don't send back the big one with the red beard. He fixed my door.*

*I teach at a school. I can give a lesson. They don't have to pay.*

Olga read the messages aloud. Svetlana listened with her arms crossed. Finally, she sighed.

“Fine,” she said. “We don't have to agree with the city. But we must give the city a better answer.”

Olga took a pen and wrote on the back of a pancake menu:

**SOCIAL REINTEGRATION COURSE FOR MEN FROM THE FUTURE.**
*Duration: two weeks. Final exam obligatory. Those who fail go home.*

Alex read it over her shoulder.

“Two weeks?” he said. “It took me a whole year.”

“You didn't have Svetlana,” Olga said.

### 2 Грамматика

### Must / have to / don't have to

Must и have to — «надо, обязан». Must not (mustn't) — «нельзя». Don't have to — «не обязательно, не нужно». You must not eat the seats. You don't have to worry. Выберите правильный вариант.

1. Registration is obligatory. All men (must / don't have to) go to the stadium.

2. You (mustn't / don't have to) eat the seats.

3. Lunch is free. The men (have to / don't have to) pay for it.

4. The people of Moscow (have to / mustn't) choose one option.

5. The teacher said: “They (don't have to / mustn't) pay for the lesson.”

6. Those who fail the exam (have to / don't have to) go home.
