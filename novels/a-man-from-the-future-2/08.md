### School No. 57 · June 23, 2025

Of three thousand men, four hundred and twelve signed up for the course. The other men started packing their machines and complained that Moscow had too many trees.

The course took place in an old school. In Classroom 1, there were thirty desks, and at each desk sat a very large man in a very small chair. On the board, Olga wrote in big letters:

**LESSON 1: A WOMAN IS NOT A SOUVENIR.**

Svetlana stood in front of the class. She was the main teacher. Nobody voted for this. Svetlana simply arrived at seven in the morning with a whistle, and nobody dared to argue.

“Good morning, class,” she said.

“GOOD MORNING, WOMAN,” answered thirty voices.

Svetlana blew the whistle. “Lesson one, rule one. You shouldn't call me ‘woman’. You should call me by my name.”

Boris raised his hand. “What is your name?”

“Svetlana Petrovna.”

Boris wrote it in his notebook. It took him four minutes.

“Rule two,” Svetlana continued. “If a woman says no, it means no. Not ‘maybe’. Not ‘ask again in five minutes’. Not ‘ask her sister’. No.”

Gleb raised his hand. “What if she says ‘maybe’?”

“Then it means maybe. You should wait. You shouldn't ask every five minutes.”

“How long should I wait?”

“Until she tells you.”

There was a horrified murmur in the class. In 3024, nobody waited for anything. Everything came from a screen in ten seconds.

In Classroom 2, Nina was teaching a lesson called **FOOD AND FLOWERS: A BIG DIFFERENCE**. On her table, there were roses, tulips, pancakes, and a carrot.

“Which of these can you eat?” she asked.

Fedya pointed at everything.

“No,” said Nina patiently. “Flowers you give. Food you eat. You shouldn't eat a present.”

“But what if the present looks delicious?”

“Then you should say ‘thank you’ and put it in water.”

Fedya looked at the roses with great sadness.

In Classroom 3, Alex taught the hardest subject of all: **FEELINGS**.

“Okay,” he said. “Today we're going to talk about our feelings. Who wants to start?”

Thirty men looked at him in silence. One of them slowly put his hand up.

“Is it a technical problem?”

“No. A feeling is… when something happens inside you. When you're happy, sad, angry, nervous.”

“In 3024,” said a man in the back row, “we have one feeling. It's called ‘okay’.”

“You should try to find more,” Alex said. “For example — Boris. How do you feel when you look at the sea?”

Boris closed his eyes and thought for a long time.

“Wet,” he said.

“Before you go in.”

Boris thought again. Something changed on his big red face.

“Small,” he said quietly. “But in a good way.”

The class went quiet. Alex smiled.

“That's a feeling, Boris. That's exactly a feeling.”

Boris opened his eyes. He looked very surprised and a little frightened.

“Should I report it?” he asked.

In the last lesson of the day, Olga taught **COMPLIMENTS**. It was a disaster.

“You have very strong bones,” Gleb told the volunteer. “Excellent for carrying things.”

“Your face has a good number of eyes,” said another man.

“You remind me of my favorite machine,” said Fedya, with great love.

Olga covered her face with her hands.

“You shouldn't compare a woman to a machine,” she said. “Ever. You should say something kind and true. Something simple.”

Fedya thought very hard. Then he looked at the volunteer — a quiet student called Masha, who was helping with the course.

“You have a nice laugh,” he said. “When you laughed this morning, I forgot to eat my pancake.”

Masha blushed. Olga put down her hands and stared at Fedya.

“Fedya,” she said. “That was perfect.”

Fedya looked at his pancake. It was still on his plate, untouched.

“I know,” he said. “I'm also very surprised.”

### Should / shouldn't

1. You _______ call a woman “woman”.

2. You _______ wait when she says “maybe”.

3. You _______ eat flowers.

4. You _______ say “thank you” for a present.

5. You _______ compare a woman to a machine.

6. You _______ say something kind and true.
