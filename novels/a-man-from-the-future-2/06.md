### Nina's Café · June 20, 2025 · 22:00

That night, Nina closed the café early and covered the windows with newspapers. Outside, men in silver suits were walking up and down the street, singing a song that they learned from a car radio. They only knew one line: “Baby, baby, baby, oh!” They were singing it like a military march.

Inside, around the biggest table, sat the most unusual planning team in Moscow: Alex, Olga, Nina, Svetlana — and Gleb, Fedya, and Boris.

“Why are *they* here?” Svetlana asked.

“Because they know how the others think,” Alex said. “And because Boris refused to leave.”

“I am not leaving,” Boris confirmed. “There is cake here.”

Olga put a big sheet of paper on the table and drew three circles.

“Okay. Problem one: three thousand men are walking around Moscow. Problem two: they don't listen to anybody. Problem three: we don't know what to do with them. Alex, you're the expert. How do we get them all in one place?”

Alex thought for a long time.

“Men from 3024 love two things,” he said finally. “Free food and official rules. In 3024, every day starts with a message on the screen: *All workers must go to the station.* Nobody asks why. Everybody goes.”

“So we're going to give them an order,” said Olga.

“We're going to give them an order *and* lunch.”

Gleb was writing everything in his notebook. “I can help,” he said. “I'm going to write the message in official 3024 style. They're going to believe it.”

“And I'm going to cook,” said Nina. “If they're hungry, they're going to come. Every man in the universe comes for pancakes.”

“How many pancakes?” asked Fedya with great interest.

Nina looked at Boris. Boris looked at the cake.

“A lot of pancakes,” she said.

Svetlana stood up. “Where are we going to put three thousand men?”

“Luzhniki Stadium,” said Olga. “It's big enough, it has screens, and the mayor is going to give it to us. He owes Alex.”

“He doesn't owe me anything.”

“He made you the official expert. Now he owes you a stadium.”

Alex laughed. For the first time that day, he felt that the plan was possible.

“Okay,” he said. “So tomorrow morning, we're going to show the message on every screen in Moscow. The men are going to come to Luzhniki. Nina is going to feed them. And then—”

“And then what?” asked Svetlana.

Everyone looked at Alex. Alex looked at the paper. The third circle was still empty.

“And then,” Olga said slowly, “we're going to ask the city.”

“Ask the city what?”

“What to do with them. It's not our decision. It's not Alex's decision, or the mayor's. These men came for the women of Moscow. So the people of Moscow are going to decide.”

She wrote three words in the third circle:

**SOCIALIZE. ADAPT. SEND BACK.**

“A vote,” said Svetlana. She sat down again. A slow smile appeared on her face. “I like votes.”

Fedya raised his hand.

“Excuse me. What is a vote?”

“It's when everybody chooses,” said Olga, “and the biggest group wins.”

Fedya's face became very bright. “So women are going to choose? Choose us?”

“Women,” said Svetlana, “are going to choose what to *do* with you. That's different.”

Fedya thought about it.

“I'm going to wash my face,” he decided. “Just in case.”
