### School No. 57 · July 7, 2025

The graduation ceremony took place in the school gym. There were balloons, a long table with pancakes, and a banner that Olga painted in the night: **CONGRATULATIONS, GENTLEMEN OF THE FUTURE!**

Two hundred and six men sat in rows in their best clothes. Most of them didn't have best clothes, so Nina lent them her late husband's ties. There were only thirty ties, so the men took turns.

Svetlana walked onto the stage. The whole gym stood up without being asked.

“Sit down,” she said. Everybody sat down at the same time, with a sound like a falling cupboard.

“Two weeks ago,” Svetlana began, “you arrived in our city like a herd of hungry bison. If you had come one by one, it would have been easier. But no. You came all together, on the same day, in silver suits. You ate our flowers. You proposed to a giraffe.”

“She didn't say no,” somebody said quietly from the back.

*Whistle.*

“Today,” Svetlana continued, “you are different. Not perfect. If you were perfect, I would be very suspicious. But different.” She looked at Boris. “Some of you have even learned to feel things.”

Boris wiped his eyes with Nina's late husband's tie.

Then the certificates. Olga called each man by his name — his name, not his number. When Gleb came up to the stage, the gym clapped, and a librarian in the third row clapped louder than everyone. When Fedya came up, Masha shouted “Bravo!” and he dropped his certificate twice.

Alex was the last to speak. He looked at the men in their borrowed ties.

“A year ago,” he said, “I was sitting in a gray room, and I thought that love was something you could find. Like a key in the street. If I had known how difficult it was, maybe I would never have come.” He looked at Olga in the front row. “And that would have been the biggest mistake of my life.”

“Now some of you are going to stay here. Some of you are going back to 3024 to teach the others. If I were you, I'd remember three things.”

He counted on his fingers.

“One: a woman is not a souvenir. Two: flowers are not food. Three: if she says ‘maybe’, you wait. And while you're waiting, you become a better person. That's the secret. Nobody wrote it in my diary, because I didn't know it yet.”

The gym was silent. Then two hundred and six men stood up and applauded — not in perfect rows, not at the same time, but loudly, messily, and with feeling.

***

In the evening, the machines started to leave. One by one, white lights flashed over Luzhniki, and with each *POP*, another man went home — not to a gray world this time, but to a city where women were coming back from the Moon, carrying gardens and music and very strict rules.

Svetlana left too. Irina invited her to be the Chief Teacher of the Course in 3024. She took her whistle, three boxes of notebooks, and Boris.

“Only for one year,” she said at the door of the machine. “If they behave badly, I'll come back. If they behave well, I'll come back too. I don't like their food.”

Boris carried her suitcase into the machine. Then he came back out, picked a flower from Nina's window box, and gave it to her.

He didn't eat it. Nobody even had to remind him.

The machine flashed and disappeared.

Gleb stayed in Moscow and got a job at the library. Fedya stayed too. He works in Nina's café now and makes the best pancakes in the city, although he still eats about half of them.

And Alex and Olga walked home along Tverskaya Street, past the shops and the cars and the singing neighbors, through an ordinary summer evening.

“If you could change one thing about this week,” Olga asked, “what would it be?”

Alex thought about it.

“I would hide my diary better.”

Olga laughed. “If you hid your diary better, Svetlana would never meet Boris. Your mother would still be on the Moon. And the giraffe would be single.”

“True,” said Alex. “I'll write a new one, then.”

“And what will be on the first page?”

Alex took her hand.

“Rule number one,” he said. “Never, ever take a woman to the future without asking.”

“And rule number two?”

“Ask her anyway. Maybe she wants to see the Moon.”

Olga stopped and looked at him for a long moment.

“Maybe,” she said.

And Alex — for the first time in his life — was perfectly happy to wait.

**THE END**

### Second Conditional

Второй тип условных предложений описывает нереальную или маловероятную ситуацию: If + Past Simple, would + глагол. If you were perfect, I would be suspicious. If I were you, I'd remember three things. С I / he / she обычно используют were. Поставьте глаголы в нужную форму.

1. If you _______ (be) perfect, I _______ (be) very suspicious.

2. If I _______ (be) you, I _______ (remember) three things.

3. If Alex _______ (hide) his diary better, Svetlana _______ (not / meet) Boris.

4. If I _______ (can) change one thing, I _______ (change) the diary.

5. What _______ you _______ (do) if three thousand men _______ (come) to your city?
