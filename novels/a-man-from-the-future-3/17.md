### Ostankino · Friday · 20:15

The Ostankino Tower rises more than five hundred metres above Moscow, like a giant needle stitching the clouds to the city. At its foot is the television centre: long, grey buildings full of studios, offices, corridors, and people running in every direction with coffee and headphones.

Although Alex had been in many strange places that week, this was the one that frightened him most. It was the centre of the lie. And he was walking straight into it.

They arrived separately, as Olga had planned.

At 20:15, a delivery boy arrived at the staff entrance with forty boxes of pancakes. It was Fedya, wearing a cap that said *NINA'S CAFÉ — WE DELIVER HAPPINESS*. The guard checked his list.

“Pancakes? Nobody ordered pancakes.”

“Somebody always orders pancakes,” said Fedya with complete confidence. “Usually, they just don't know it yet.”

The guard opened one box. The smell was so wonderful that his face changed. Despite the rules, despite his foil hat, and despite his diet, he took one. Then a second. Then he waved Fedya through.

Behind Fedya, carrying a large make-up case, walked a young woman with short dark hair. The guard didn't even look at her ID. He had pancake on his chin.

At 20:20, a grey, boring man in a grey, boring suit walked in through the technicians' entrance. He showed a pass that Gleb had printed in the library that afternoon. It said *Technical Department — Computers*. The guard on that door was arguing with a broken card reader.

“It's broken again,” the guard said. “Third time this week.”

The grey man opened his briefcase, took out a tiny screwdriver, and fixed it in four seconds. The guard looked at him with deep respect.

“Go in,” he said. “Go anywhere you like.”

However, Kennet had the most difficult task. He wasn't going in through any door. He was going in where the trucks went.

For three days, Kennet had been watching the grey trucks. He knew that they arrived every hour, that they used a ramp at the back of the building, and that the ramp went down — deep down — under the ground. He also knew that the drivers always stopped at the top of the ramp for exactly two minutes to check their papers.

At 20:30, a grey *MOSCOW FRESH AIR* truck stopped at the top of the ramp. In spite of his size, Kennet moved silently, like he did in the Scottish forests when he followed deer. While the driver was checking his papers, he climbed underneath the truck and held on to the frame with his enormous hands.

The truck drove down. Level −1. Level −2. Level −3.

The air became cold. The walls became bare concrete. And then the truck stopped, and Kennet heard a sound he had never heard before: a deep, low hum, like a fridge the size of a house, and under it, faintly, thousands of tiny sounds — giggles, chuckles, laughs — all trapped and all very, very quiet.

***

Meanwhile, upstairs, Studio 5 was filling up.

The studio audience was huge: three hundred seats, and every one of them was taken. Many people in the audience were wearing foil hats. Some had brought signs: *CATCH THE ALIEN!* and *HUMANS FIRST!* But if you looked carefully, you could see other faces too. Zoya from the market, in her best dress. The zookeeper, with his white beard. The old man from the banya, with his umbrella-carrying wife. The busker with his accordion. The students, whose foil hats had flowers on them. Gleb and Vera, holding hands. Rimma, with her can of hairspray. Nina, who had somehow brought a bag of pancakes past four security guards.

And in the third row, in a severe grey jacket and glasses, sat a woman who looked like she worked at a ministry. Nobody recognized her as Olga.

On the stage, a large chair stood under a spotlight. Behind it was a huge screen showing the famous “spaceship” picture.

At 20:55, the lights went down. Dramatic music began. And Doctor Gennady Grumov walked onto the stage.

The audience went silent. Even though most of them knew the plan, even though they were among friends, there was something about Grumov that made the air colder. He stood very still under the light, thin and pale, his bald head shining, his tiny moustache perfectly straight.

“Good evening, citizens,” he said. “Tonight is the night we have been waiting for.”

Olga, in the third row, quietly checked her phone. One message from Lucy: *In the control room. Looking for the file.* Nothing from Kennet.

Nothing from Alex.

Where was Alex?

### 2 Грамматика

### Связки: although, however, despite / in spite of

Although / even though + предложение: Although Alex had been in many strange places, this one frightened him most. However — в начале нового предложения, после него запятая: However, Kennet had the most difficult task. Despite / in spite of + существительное или -ing: Despite the rules, he took one. In spite of his size, Kennet moved silently. Соедините предложения с помощью указанной связки.

1. The guard had a list. He let Fedya in. (although)

2. The guard was on a diet. He ate two pancakes. (despite)

3. Kennet is very big. He moved silently. (in spite of)

4. Alex and Lucy got in easily. Kennet had a harder job. (however)

5. They were among friends. Grumov made the air colder. (even though)

6. It was very cold. Kennet held on to the truck. (despite)
