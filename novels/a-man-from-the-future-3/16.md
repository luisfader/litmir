### Rimma's Hair Salon · Friday Morning

“The suit is good,” said Olga, “but your face is still your face. And your hair is on every screen in Moscow.”

So on Friday morning, they went to a small hair salon in a basement near Chistye Prudy. It belonged to Rimma — an old friend of Nina's, a small woman with purple hair, enormous earrings, and the fastest scissors in the city. She had cut Nina's hair for twenty-five years.

“Sit,” said Rimma, pointing at a chair with her comb. “Don't talk. I'll work it out myself.”

Rimma talked non-stop. She talked while she washed Alex's hair. She talked while she cut it. She talked about her daughter, her neighbour, her neighbour's daughter, the price of tomatoes, and the man on television who said there was an alien in Moscow.

“Can you believe it?” she said, cutting quickly. “An alien! In Moscow! Where would he even live? Have you seen the rents?” Snip, snip. “My neighbour has already called the police three times. First she reported the postman. Then she reported her own husband. Then she reported her cat.” Snip. “The police told her to calm down. She told them to get lost. They hung up on her.”

Alex looked at Olga in the mirror. Olga shook her head very slightly: *don't say anything.*

“Personally,” Rimma went on, “I don't believe it. Aliens would never come to Moscow. Too much traffic.” She turned the chair around. “Now. Dark brown or black?”

“Sorry?”

“Your hair, darling. You can't walk around with this colour. Everybody's looking for this colour. Dark brown, I think. And we'll put on a little beard, too. A fake one. Very elegant.”

An hour later, Alex looked in the mirror and didn't recognize himself. His hair was short and dark. He had a small, neat beard. With the grey suit and the briefcase, he looked like a man who had never done anything interesting in his whole life.

“Rimma,” said Nina, “you're a genius.”

“I know,” said Rimma. “Now, who's next?”

Lucy sat down in the chair. Rimma pinned up her long pale hair under a short dark wig. Then she did Olga's hair in a way that made her look ten years older and very serious, like a woman who worked in a ministry.

While they were waiting, Olga explained the plan. She had been working it out all night.

“Grumov's live show starts at nine,” she said. “He's going to say he can ‘catch the alien’. He'll try to keep everybody afraid. So we're going to give him what he wants.”

“What does he want?” asked Kennet.

“An alien.” Olga looked at Alex. “At the end of the show, Alex is going to walk onto the stage and give himself up.”

“*What?*” said everyone except Olga.

“Not really give up. That's only the beginning. While everyone is looking at Alex, Lucy will get into the control room. She's going to find the original file of the ‘spaceship’ picture on Grumov's computer and take off the layer of shadow. If the cat is there, she'll put the real picture on every screen in Moscow. Live.”

“How will I get into the control room?” asked Lucy.

“You'll be a make-up artist. They always need make-up artists. And you're an artist, so you'll know what to do with the brushes.”

“And me?” asked Kennet.

“You'll find the machine. You've followed the trucks. You know which entrance they use. Find the Moodvac, and find out how to switch it off.”

“And me?” asked Alex.

Olga smiled.

“You're going to do the most difficult thing of all,” she said. “You're going to make people laugh. On live television. Together.”

Alex went pale under his new beard.

“Olga. I'm not funny. You know I'm not funny. In the metro, nobody laughed at my joke.”

“One person did,” said Olga. “And that was enough to stop the machine. Tonight, we'll have more than one.” She took out her phone. “I've already called a few people.”

She showed them the screen. It was a group chat called *TONIGHT AT NINE*. There were already sixty-three people in it: Zoya from the market. The old zookeeper. Gleb and Vera. The busker from the metro. The students with the decorated foil hats. The actor from the planetarium. The old man from the banya. Fedya. Rimma's neighbour (“the one who reported her cat”, said Rimma). And dozens of others: people who had seen Alex being chased all week and had decided that nobody so bad at hiding could possibly be dangerous.

“They've all got tickets for the studio audience,” said Olga. “Grumov sold them online. He wants as many people as possible to watch him catch the alien.”

“So the studio will be full,” said Kennet slowly, “of our friends.”

“Full of people,” said Olga, “who are ready to laugh.”

Rimma put down her scissors.

“I don't understand anything,” she said. “But I'm coming too.” She picked up a can of hairspray as if it were a weapon. “Nobody tells Moscow it can't laugh. Not while I'm cutting its hair.”
