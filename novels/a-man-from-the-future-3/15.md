### A Shopping Centre · Thursday

“If we're going to get into Ostankino on Friday,” said Olga the next morning, “we need disguises. Proper ones. Not a cap and a T-shirt.”

So they went to one of Moscow's biggest shopping centres: five floors of shops, escalators, fountains, a food court that smelled of fried chicken and coffee, and a cinema on the top floor. It was so big that people got lost in it every day, which made it perfect for hiding.

Kennet took Alex to the men's department. Olga and Lucy went to the women's floor. Nina went to the food court and said she would “guard the pancakes”.

For the first half hour, Olga and Lucy were very polite to each other. Too polite. They looked at jackets. They looked at scarves. They said things like “That's a nice colour” and “Yes, very nice”. It was such an awkward conversation that a shop assistant asked them if they were sisters who had had an argument.

Then Lucy went into a fitting room with a long black dress. After a minute, she called out:

“Olga? Could you help me with the zip? It's too high. I can't reach it.”

Olga went in. The fitting room was so small that they had to stand very close together. Olga pulled up the zip. In the mirror, Lucy looked like a film star — and also like a person who was about to cry.

“Are you all right?” asked Olga.

Lucy sat down on the small stool in the fitting room.

“Can I tell you something?” she said. “I've never told anyone.”

“…Yes.”

“I've been in love with Kennet for six years.”

Olga stared at her.

“With *Kennet*?”

“Since the first day of art school. He was the model. Everybody was drawing his shoulders, because he has such enormous shoulders. But I looked at his face, and it was such a kind face that I couldn't draw anything else.” Lucy laughed sadly. “And for six years he has carried my easel up and down every staircase in Europe, and he has never said a word. He's too shy. And I'm too much of a coward.”

Olga sat down on the floor of the fitting room. She started to laugh. She couldn't stop.

“What's funny?” asked Lucy, hurt.

“Nothing. Me. I'm funny.” Olga wiped her eyes. “Lucy, I have to confess something. Since the day you arrived, I've been jealous of you. Terribly jealous. I hid the portrait you drew of Alex in a cupboard. I broke two pencils. I nearly told you that Alex's ears were none of your business.”

Lucy's mouth fell open. Then she started to laugh too.

“Jealous of *me*? Olga — your husband talks about you all the time! When we were in Gorky Park, he spent twenty minutes explaining how you draw cats in your pictures. He's so in love with you that it's almost embarrassing. I've been jealous of *you*.”

They looked at each other in the mirror: two women in a tiny fitting room, laughing so loudly that the shop assistant knocked on the door to check if everyone was alive.

“We're fine!” called Olga. “We're just being stupid!”

When they finally calmed down, Lucy said: “He's not brave enough to say anything, you know. Kennet. He'll carry my easel until we're both ninety.”

“Then maybe you should say something.”

“I'm not brave enough either.”

Olga thought about it.

“We're going to break into a television centre tomorrow,” she said. “If you're brave enough for that, you're brave enough for anything.”

Lucy smiled — a real smile this time.

***

Meanwhile, on the third floor, the men's shopping was not going so well.

Kennet had chosen a disguise for Alex: a long brown coat, a hat with a wide brim, and a pair of round glasses. When Alex came out of the fitting room, the shop assistant said that he looked like a detective from an old film.

“Too obvious,” said Kennet. “Try this.”

The second disguise was a tracksuit, white trainers, and a gold chain. Alex looked like a man who sells second-hand cars.

“Not good enough,” said Kennet.

The third disguise was a grey suit and a tie, with a briefcase. Alex looked exactly like a television technician.

Kennet nodded slowly.

“That's it,” he said. “You're such a typical office worker now that you're invisible.”

Alex looked at himself in the mirror. He was so boring that he could hardly see himself.

“Perfect,” he said.

They met the women in the food court, where Nina had eaten four pancakes and was “testing” a fifth. When Lucy saw Kennet, she suddenly blushed and looked at the floor. Kennet blushed too and dropped all the shopping bags.

Olga and Alex looked at each other over the table.

“Did something happen?” whispered Alex.

“Something is *going* to happen,” whispered Olga. “Eat your pancake.”

### 2 Грамматика

### So / such, too / enough

So + прилагательное: so small; such + (a) + прилагательное + существительное: such an awkward conversation. Конструкция so/such … that показывает результат. Too + прилагательное = «слишком» (плохо): too high. Прилагательное + enough = «достаточно»: brave enough. Enough + существительное: enough time. Заполните пропуски: so, such, too или enough.

1. It was _______ an awkward conversation that a shop assistant thought they were sisters.

2. The fitting room was _______ small that they stood very close.

3. The zip is _______ high. I can't reach it.

4. He has _______ a kind face that I couldn't draw anything else.

5. He's not brave _______ to say anything.

6. That disguise is _______ obvious. Try another one.
