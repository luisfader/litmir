### Moscow · The Next Day

By the next morning, Moscow had gone completely mad.

It started in the supermarkets. At eight o'clock, a woman told a shop assistant that she needed aluminium foil — “a lot of aluminium foil, for my head”. By ten o'clock, there was no foil left in the city. People were wearing it as hats, as scarves, and in one case as a full suit. A man on the metro said that the foil blocked “alien brain waves”. Another man said that it also kept his sandwiches fresh.

At work, colleagues looked at each other suspiciously. In one office, the manager announced that everybody had to show their ears, because “aliens have different ears”. In a school, the children told their teacher that their maths teacher was definitely an alien, because nobody normal could understand fractions. On the internet, a woman said that her cat had looked at her “in a non-Earth way”.

And everywhere, people were talking about Alex.

In the café, Nina had put a sign on the door: CLOSED FOR TECHNICAL REASONS. Inside, the curtains were shut. Alex was sitting at a table with his head in his hands.

“I don't understand,” he said. “I'm not an alien. I'm just from the future. Why don't people believe me?”

“Because ‘just from the future’ isn't a very normal sentence either,” said Olga.

“Then I'll prove I'm human.” Alex stood up. He looked determined. “I'll do human things. In public. Everybody will see.”

Olga, Nina, Lucy, and Kennet exchanged looks.

“What kind of human things?” Olga asked carefully.

The answer was: a lot of them, and all of them went wrong.

First, Alex went to the bakery across the street and bought a loaf of bread. He told the baker that he was eating bread “like all humans do”. The baker said that he had never seen an alien eat bread so fast, and he called his wife to watch.

Then Alex sat on a bench in the park and ate a bowl of borscht that Nina had made. A crowd gathered. An old man said that real humans didn't eat soup on park benches. A young woman said that the soup was red, and that red was “a very alien colour”. A journalist asked Alex whether borscht was the fuel for his spaceship.

Then Alex sang a famous Russian song in the middle of the street, to show that he knew human culture. Unfortunately, his singing was terrible. A little boy told his mother that the alien was “calling his friends from space”. Several people ran away. A dog howled in agreement.

Finally, Alex went to a clinic and asked a doctor to examine him. “Please tell everybody that I'm completely normal,” he said. The doctor listened to his heart and looked into his ears. Then she said that his heart rate was “suspiciously calm” and that his ears were “unusually clean”. She took a photo of his ears and posted it online.

By four o'clock, the photo of Alex's ears had two million views.

Alex came back to the café and lay down on the floor.

“They said my ears were suspicious,” he told the ceiling.

Lucy sat down next to him on the floor, with her tea. “For what it's worth,” she said, “I think they're very nice ears. Very human. I could draw them.”

“Please don't draw his ears,” said Olga, a little too fast.

Kennet, who had been standing by the window, turned around. He had been watching the street for an hour without moving, the way he watched forests when he went walking in Scotland.

“There's something strange out there,” he said. “People are angry. But it isn't normal angry. It's like their good mood is… leaking out of them.”

“What do you mean?”

Kennet pointed. Down the street, a grey truck with dark windows was driving slowly past the café. On its side, in big friendly letters, it said: *MOSCOW FRESH AIR — CLEANING THE CITY FOR YOU*. On its roof was something that looked like a giant vacuum cleaner.

As the truck passed, a couple who had been laughing together on the corner suddenly stopped laughing. The man frowned. The woman told him that he was always late and walked away.

“That's the fourth one I've seen today,” said Kennet quietly.

Before anyone could answer, there was a loud knock at the door.

“Police!” said a voice. “Open up! We have been told that an alien lives here!”

### 2 Грамматика

### Косвенная речь: утверждения

В косвенной речи после глагола в прошедшем времени (said, told) время обычно «сдвигается назад»: is → was, are → were, Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, will → would. Также меняются местоимения: I → he/she, my → his/her. Said не требует дополнения, told — требует: She said (that)… / She told him (that)… Переведите прямую речь в косвенную.

1. A woman: “I need a lot of aluminium foil.” → A woman told the shop assistant that …

2. A man: “The foil blocks alien brain waves.” → A man said that …

3. The baker: “I have never seen an alien eat bread so fast.” → The baker said that …

4. A little boy: “The alien is calling his friends.” → A little boy told his mother that …

5. The doctor: “His ears are unusually clean.” → The doctor said that …

6. Alex: “I will prove I'm human.” → Alex said that …
