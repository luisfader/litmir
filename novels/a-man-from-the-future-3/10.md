### Gorky Park

In the morning, after a night of sleeping on a bench at a closed metro station and being woken up by a cleaner with a very loud floor machine, they went to Gorky Park.

Gorky Park is the kind of place where Moscow goes to relax. There are long paths along the river, flower beds, fountains, cafés, a pond with rowing boats, and people everywhere: cycling, skateboarding, rollerblading, jogging, doing yoga, walking dogs, and pretending to jog while actually eating ice cream.

“The best place to hide is in a crowd,” said Lucy. “And this is the biggest crowd in Moscow.”

Alex was wearing a baseball cap, sunglasses, and a T-shirt that said *I ♥ MOSCOW*, which Kennet had bought from a souvenir stall. He looked exactly like a tourist, which was the plan.

Olga had been thinking all night about what had happened in the metro. While the others were buying coffee, she sat on a bench and wrote in a notebook, fast, crossing things out.

*The machine takes good mood. But not ALL good mood. When people laugh together, it can't take it. Why? Shared laughter = stronger? If we could make the whole city laugh together at the same time…*

She stopped writing. She looked up — and saw Alex and Lucy at the edge of the pond.

Lucy had set up her easel and was painting the boats. Alex was standing next to her, holding her brushes while she worked. She said something, and he laughed. Then she laughed. Then she touched his arm — just for a second — to point at a duck.

Olga closed her notebook.

“Alex,” she called. “Can you come here, please?”

Alex came over, still smiling. “Lucy was telling me about English ducks. Apparently they're very polite. They queue.”

“How lovely,” said Olga. “Do you enjoy spending time with her?”

Alex didn't notice the danger. Men from 3024 never notice the danger. It's not a skill they have.

“Yes! She's really interesting. She's travelled everywhere. She keeps forgetting to eat because she's busy painting. And she says I have a good eye for colour.”

“Does she.”

“Yes. She wants me to try painting. She says I should stop worrying about making mistakes.”

“I've been telling you to stop worrying for a whole year,” said Olga.

“Have you?”

Olga stood up. “Let's take a boat,” she said. “Just the two of us. I need to talk to you.”

They hired a rowing boat. Alex had never rowed before, so the first ten minutes were spent going round in circles while a group of ducks watched them with interest. Finally, Olga took the oars herself.

In the middle of the pond, she stopped rowing.

“Alex. Do you like Lucy?”

“Of course. Everybody likes Lucy.”

“That's not what I mean.”

Alex looked at her. Slowly — very slowly — he began to understand. He remembered Svetlana's lessons last summer: *When a woman asks a simple question, it's never a simple question.*

“Olga,” he said carefully. “Are you… jealous?”

“No,” said Olga. She looked at the ducks. “Yes. A bit. A lot. I don't know.” She sighed. “She's beautiful, Alex. She's talented. She drinks tea in a way that looks like a film. She knows about art, and you've started talking about art. You've stopped noticing that I also draw.”

Alex was quiet for a long moment.

“Olga,” he said. “In 3024, I spent twenty years imagining a woman. I imagined everything — her face, her voice, her laugh. When I met you, you were completely different from what I'd imagined. You were better. Because you were real.” He paused. “Lucy is very nice. But she's like a beautiful painting in a museum. I enjoy looking at it. I don't want to live in it.”

Olga didn't say anything. But her shoulders relaxed.

“And,” Alex added, “I've never stopped noticing that you draw. I just didn't want to say anything, because you always hide a little cat in every picture, and I thought it was a secret.”

Olga turned around very quickly.

“You noticed the cats?”

“Of course. There's one in every illustration. In the children's book you did last year, there's a cat on every page. One is hiding behind the time machine.”

Olga stared at him. Then her eyes went wide.

“Behind the time machine,” she repeated. “Alex — the picture on Grumov's show. The spaceship. I *knew* it looked familiar. It's not a photo. It's *my illustration*. From my book. He's added some silver light to it, but it's mine.”

“Are you sure?”

“There's only one way to know.” She grabbed the oars and began to row towards the shore so fast that the ducks had to jump out of the way. “If it's my picture, there's a cat in it.”

On the shore, Lucy was waving at them. She had seen their faces and had already understood that something had changed.

### 2 Грамматика

### Герундий или инфинитив

После одних глаголов идёт герундий (-ing): enjoy, stop, keep, finish, mind, avoid. После других — инфинитив с to: want, decide, need, hope, learn, agree. После предлогов всегда герундий: busy painting, worry about making. Поставьте глагол в правильную форму.

1. Do you enjoy _______ (spend) time with her?

2. Lucy keeps _______ (forget) to eat.

3. She wants me _______ (try) painting.

4. You should stop _______ (worry) about _______ (make) mistakes.

5. Olga decided _______ (take) a boat.

6. Alex had never learned _______ (row).
