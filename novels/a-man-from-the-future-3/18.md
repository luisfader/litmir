### Ostankino · Level −3 · 20:50

Alex was in the basement.

He hadn't planned to be. He had been walking through a corridor on the technical floor, looking for the stairs to Studio 5, when he had passed an open door and heard something. A sound. Very faint. Thousands of tiny laughs, trapped somewhere below him.

He had followed it. Down a staircase. Then another. Then a third. And now he was standing in a huge, cold room in front of the strangest machine he had ever seen.

The Moodvac 3000 was made of copper pipes and glass tubes that glowed with a soft silver light. Dials on its front showed numbers that went up and up. And all along the walls, on metal shelves, stood thousands of little glass bottles, each one filled with a glowing silvery cloud.

Alex walked slowly along the shelves, reading the labels.

*Bride laughing at her own wedding. Gorky Park. 15:10.*

*Old man winning at chess. Sokolniki. 12:00.*

*Little girl and grandmother. Tickling. Tverskaya. 17:45.*

His throat felt tight. These weren't just bottles. They were moments. Thousands of small, happy moments that had been stolen from people who didn't even know they had lost them.

“Alex.”

He jumped. Kennet was standing behind a truck at the back of the room, covered in dust.

“You found it too,” whispered Kennet.

“How do we switch it off?”

They went to the control panel. Alex studied it. It was complicated, old-fashioned, and very badly designed — whoever had built it had used factory equipment from the 1970s and had connected it all with sticky tape. But it was a machine. And Alex understood machines.

“I can't just switch it off,” he said quietly. “If I do, the bottles stay closed. The good mood stays trapped. We need to reverse it. Make it blow instead of suck.”

“Can you do that?”

“Give me ten minutes.”

He had five.

“How very interesting,” said a voice like an unoiled door.

Doctor Grumov was standing at the entrance, in his television suit. Behind him stood Tolik, the nervous assistant, and four large security guards.

“I had the cameras installed last month,” said Grumov. “Every corner of this room. I saw you on the monitor as soon as you came in. A technician with a beard. Very clever. Your wife must have had your hair done.”

He walked towards them slowly.

“You know, I didn't expect you to come to me. I expected to have you found by the police. But this is so much better.” His moustache moved slightly. “Guards — have him taken upstairs. And have the big one locked in the truck.”

Two guards took Kennet's arms. It took them four tries. Kennet didn't fight; he just looked at Alex calmly, as if to say: *Not yet.*

Two other guards took Alex.

“Where are you taking me?” asked Alex.

“To the studio, of course,” said Grumov. “It's my special live show. I promised the city an alien. And now I have one.” He checked his watch. “I'll have you put in the chair under the spotlight. I'll have the cameras focus on your face. And all of Moscow will be so frightened that the trucks won't be able to carry all the bottles.”

As the guards pulled him towards the door, Alex looked back at the machine — at the control panel he had almost understood, at the dial that showed the pressure inside the tank.

“Doctor Grumov,” he said. “Can I ask you one question?”

Grumov stopped.

“Why do you want everyone's happiness? You can't keep it. It only lasts thirty seconds. I saw your notes.”

For a moment, something moved across Grumov's face. Something small and old and very sad.

“Because thirty seconds,” he said quietly, “is thirty seconds more than I've ever had.”

Then his face became grey and still again.

“Take him upstairs.”

***

In Studio 5, at exactly 21:00, the red *ON AIR* light came on.

Grumov walked onto the stage to dramatic music. He raised his long arms.

“Citizens! Tonight, I promised you the truth. And here it is!”

The spotlight turned. Two guards brought out a man in a grey suit with a small dark beard and pushed him into the chair.

“The alien!” cried Grumov.

The audience gasped. The people with *CATCH THE ALIEN* signs booed. And in the third row, a severe woman in glasses felt her heart stop.

*This wasn't the plan.*

Olga looked at the man in the chair. Alex looked back at her. He didn't look afraid. He looked at her, and then, very slightly, he raised his eyebrows — the way he did when he had fixed something difficult and wanted her to notice.

And Olga's phone vibrated. A message from Lucy.

*Found the file. Removing the shadow now. Tell me when.*

### 2 Грамматика

### Have / get something done

Have / get + предмет + третья форма глагола: действие делает кто-то другой для нас. I had the cameras installed (кто-то установил их по моему заказу). Your wife must have had your hair done. Have him taken upstairs (пусть его отведут). Get — более разговорный вариант. Перепишите предложения с have something done.

1. A technician installed the cameras for Grumov. → Grumov had …

2. Rimma dyed Alex's hair for him. → Alex had …

3. Grumov wanted the police to find Alex. → Grumov wanted to have …

4. Grumov told the guards to lock Kennet in the truck. → Grumov had …

5. A printer printed Alex's pass for Gleb. → Gleb had …

6. Olga paid someone to make the disguises. → Olga got …
