### Nina's Café · April 2026

The two visitors from London arrived on a rainy Tuesday morning, and the first thing Olga noticed was that one of them was extremely beautiful.

Alex was standing behind the counter, fixing the coffee machine for the third time that week. Fedya was making pancakes in the kitchen and singing loudly. Nina was reading the newspaper. Olga was sitting by the window with her sketchbook, drawing a cat that was sleeping on the windowsill outside.

The door opened, and a young woman walked in, shaking the rain from her umbrella. She had long, pale-blonde hair, very light eyes, and the kind of face that made people forget what they were saying. She was wearing a long cream coat, and she was carrying a sketchbook under her arm — a sketchbook exactly like Olga's.

Behind her came a man so big that he had to bend down to get through the door. He had broad shoulders, messy brown hair, and a kind, slightly sleepy smile. He was carrying a wooden easel, two bags, and a tube for paintings, and he didn't look tired at all.

“Good morning!” said the woman in English. “I'm so sorry. Is this the café with the books? A friend told us about it.”

Nina looked up from her newspaper. Her English was slow but brave.

“Yes. Books. Coffee. Pancakes. Sit, please.”

“Wonderful.” The woman smiled, and the whole café seemed a little brighter. “I'm Lucy. This is Kennet. We've come from London. I'm an artist, and I'm going to paint Moscow for an exhibition.”

“Hello,” said Kennet. He put down the easel very carefully, as if it were a baby, and then sat down on a chair, which made a worried noise.

While Nina was taking their order, Lucy took out a small tablet and tried to turn it on. Nothing happened. She pressed the button again and frowned.

“Oh no. It died on the plane. All my reference photos are on it.”

Alex came out from behind the counter.

“May I?” he asked. “I repair computers.”

Lucy gave him the tablet. Alex opened the back with a tiny screwdriver, looked inside for about five seconds, and moved something with his fingernail. The screen lit up.

“A loose cable,” he said. “It happens when you fly.”

Lucy stared at him. “That was incredible. How did you do that so fast?”

“Practice,” said Alex. “Lots of practice. Where I come from, everything breaks.”

“Where do you come from?”

Alex opened his mouth. Olga, who was listening from the window, coughed loudly.

“From… a very small town,” said Alex. “A long way from here.”

“Well, thank you.” Lucy took out her sketchbook. “Let me pay you. Actually — let me draw you. That's how I say thank you.”

Before Alex could answer, she was already drawing. Her pencil moved quickly and lightly across the page. Alex stood very still, not knowing where to put his hands. Kennet watched with a calm, proud expression, like a man who had seen this many times before.

At the window, Olga was also drawing. She was drawing the cat. But she was pressing the pencil so hard that she broke it twice.

“Olga,” Nina said quietly in Russian, “you're making holes in the paper.”

“I'm not making holes. I'm adding texture.”

Five minutes later, Lucy turned the sketchbook around. It was a portrait of Alex, and it was very good. Somehow she had drawn exactly the expression he had when he looked at broken things — curious, patient, and a little bit happy.

“For you,” she said, tearing out the page.

“It's beautiful,” said Alex sincerely. He looked at it for a long time. Then he turned and held it up. “Olga, look! Look how she did the eyes!”

Olga looked.

“Yes,” she said. “Very nice eyes.”

Kennet, who was finishing his third pancake, looked at Olga, then at Alex, then at Lucy, and slowly raised one eyebrow. He didn't say anything. But Olga had the strong feeling that the big quiet man from London had understood everything.

That evening, at home, Alex put the portrait on the fridge with a magnet.

The next morning, Olga moved it to the inside of a kitchen cupboard.

“For protection,” she said. “From the sun.”

Alex, who was reading the news on his phone, didn't hear her. He was frowning at the screen.

“Olga,” he said. “Who is Doctor Grumov? And why is he talking about me on television?”

### 2 Грамматика

### Повествовательные времена: Past Simple и Past Continuous

В рассказе о прошлом Past Simple передаёт цепочку событий (She walked in, sat down and smiled), а Past Continuous — фон, на котором они происходят (Fedya was singing in the kitchen). Действие, которое прерывает другое, стоит в Past Simple: While Nina was taking their order, Lucy took out a tablet. Поставьте глаголы в правильную форму.

1. When the visitors _______ (arrive), Alex _______ (fix) the coffee machine.

2. Fedya _______ (make) pancakes and _______ (sing) loudly.

3. While Lucy _______ (draw), Alex _______ (stand) very still.

4. Olga _______ (break) her pencil twice while she _______ (listen).

5. Alex _______ (read) the news when he _______ (see) Doctor Grumov.

6. What _______ Olga _______ (do) when Lucy walked in?
