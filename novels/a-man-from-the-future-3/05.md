### Nina's Café · Back Door

For a second, nobody moved.

Then Nina picked up her rolling pin, Fedya picked up a frying pan, and Olga pushed Alex towards the kitchen.

“The back door,” she whispered. “Go. Now.”

“But I haven't done anything wrong!”

“That's exactly why you have to go. Innocent people always want to explain. Explaining takes hours. Hours in a police station.”

The knocking became louder. Nina walked slowly to the front door and opened it just a little, keeping the chain on.

Two police officers were standing outside. One was young and nervous. The other was older, with a large grey moustache and a tired face. Both were wearing aluminium foil under their caps.

“Good afternoon,” said the older officer. “I'm Sergeant Popov. We would like to ask you a few questions.”

“Ask,” said Nina.

“Does an alien live here?”

“No.”

“Does a man called Alex live here?”

“He visits. He's my nephew-in-law.”

“Is he here now?”

Nina thought about it very carefully. In the kitchen behind her, she could hear the sound of a very big man trying to open a very small window.

“Define ‘here’,” she said.

In the kitchen, things were not going well. The back door was locked, and nobody could find the key. Fedya said that the key was usually in the flour. Kennet looked at the small window above the sink, then at Alex.

“You'll fit,” he said. “Probably.”

“Probably?”

“I'll push.”

While Sergeant Popov was asking Nina whether her nephew-in-law had ever glowed in the dark, Kennet lifted Alex like a sack of potatoes and pushed him head first through the kitchen window. Alex got stuck halfway. His top half was in the alley behind the café. His bottom half was still in the kitchen, kicking.

“Kennet,” he said in a strangled voice, “I'm stuck.”

“I know,” said Kennet calmly. “I'm thinking.”

Lucy appeared with a bottle of sunflower oil. “Will this help?”

“Lucy,” said Olga, “please don't oil my husband.”

But there was no time to argue. In the front room, the young police officer was asking Nina if he could look in the kitchen. Nina told him that the kitchen was very dirty and dangerous. He asked her why it was dangerous. She told him that Fedya was making pancakes. The officer asked what was dangerous about pancakes. Nina asked him if he had ever met Fedya.

Kennet took a deep breath, put both hands on Alex's shoes, and pushed. With a loud *pop*, Alex flew out of the window and landed in a pile of empty cardboard boxes in the alley.

A moment later, Olga climbed out after him — elegantly, because she was much smaller. Then Lucy, with her sketchbook in her teeth. Kennet looked at the window, then at the door, then simply opened the door, because Fedya had found the key in the flour.

When the young police officer finally entered the kitchen, he found only Fedya, standing at the stove with a smile and a pancake.

“Pancake?” Fedya offered.

The officer looked at the open window. He looked at the oil on the floor. He looked at Fedya.

“Where did they go?” he asked.

Fedya thought about it.

“Where did *who* go?” he asked back. In almost a year in Moscow, it was the cleverest thing he had ever said.

***

Five minutes later, four people were running down a narrow street in the old centre of Moscow. Alex had cardboard in his hair. Olga was holding his hand. Lucy was holding her sketchbook. Kennet was carrying all the bags and wasn't even out of breath.

“Where are we going?” Alex asked.

“Somewhere with a lot of people,” said Olga, “where nobody looks at faces.”

“Where do people never look at faces?”

Lucy smiled suddenly. She pointed at a sign at the end of the street.

**THE STATE TRETYAKOV GALLERY →**

“In a museum,” she said. “Everybody looks at the paintings.”

### 2 Грамматика

### Косвенная речь: вопросы

В косвенных вопросах нет вспомогательного глагола do/does/did и порядок слов прямой, как в утверждении. Для вопросов «да/нет» используйте if или whether, для остальных — вопросительное слово. «Does he live here?» → He asked if he lived there. «Why is it dangerous?» → He asked her why it was dangerous. Переведите вопросы в косвенную речь.

1. Popov: “Does an alien live here?” → Popov asked …

2. Popov: “Is he here now?” → Popov asked …

3. The officer: “Can I look in the kitchen?” → The officer asked if …

4. The officer: “What is dangerous about pancakes?” → The officer asked …

5. Nina: “Have you ever met Fedya?” → Nina asked him …

6. Alex: “Where are we going?” → Alex asked …
