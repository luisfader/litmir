### Channel 9 · The Hour of Truth

At nine o'clock that evening, half of Moscow was watching the same television programme.

It was called *The Hour of Truth*, and the presenter was Doctor Gennady Grumov — a thin, pale man with a perfectly bald head, a tiny black moustache, and a voice like a door that somebody had forgotten to oil. Nobody knew what he was a doctor of. Nobody had ever seen him smile. Before he started his programme, he had worked as a weather forecaster, a dentist's assistant, and — according to one old newspaper — a very unsuccessful comedian.

Alex, Olga, and Nina were watching in the café after closing time. Lucy and Kennet had come too, because Lucy wanted to “see real Russian television”. Fedya was eating popcorn from a salad bowl.

“Good evening, citizens,” said Grumov. “Tonight, I will tell you the truth. A terrible truth. A truth that the government has hidden from you.”

Dramatic music. A photo of Alex appeared on the screen. It was an old photo from the newspaper — the one from last summer, when Alex had spoken at City Hall about the men from 3024.

Fedya dropped the popcorn.

“This man,” said Grumov, “calls himself Alex. He says he is from the year 3024. A year ago, three thousand strange men suddenly appeared in our city. Who brought them? *He* did. Then they suddenly disappeared. Who sent them away? *He* did.” Grumov leaned towards the camera. “But I have discovered his real secret. This man is not from the future. This man is from *another planet*.”

“What?” said Alex.

“Shh!” said everybody.

“I have collected evidence,” Grumov continued. He counted on his long, thin fingers. “One. This man has never had a cold. I have checked: no pharmacy in Moscow has ever sold him a single tissue. Two. He does not understand jokes about popular television shows. Three. Witnesses report that, on his first day in Moscow, he tried to pay for a croissant with his *wrist*.”

“That's true,” Olga admitted quietly.

“And four.” Grumov pressed a button. A new picture appeared: a strange red machine standing in a dark alley, shining with silver light. “This is his spaceship. It was photographed last year near Tverskaya Street.”

Olga leaned forward. Something about the picture looked strangely familiar, but she couldn't say what.

“This alien,” said Grumov, “has come here with one plan. He wants to steal the technology of humanity — our computers, our telephones, our coffee machines — and take them back to his planet. Has he been working with computers since he arrived? Yes. Has he been repairing them? Yes. Has he been studying them? *Yes.* Citizens, I ask you: why would a normal person be so good at fixing things?”

Nina looked at the coffee machine. It was working perfectly for the first time in five years.

“Oh dear,” she said.

“Be careful, Moscow,” Grumov finished. His voice dropped to a whisper. “Check your neighbours. Check your colleagues. Check your husbands. And remember: if somebody is too helpful… he might not be from here.”

The programme ended. For a long moment, nobody in the café spoke.

Then Kennet said, slowly and calmly: “Well. That was a load of nonsense.”

“Thank you, Kennet,” said Alex.

“But it was very well presented nonsense,” Kennet added. “That's the dangerous kind.”

Lucy had taken out her sketchbook and was looking at the picture of the “spaceship”, which was still frozen on the screen. She tilted her head.

“That's funny,” she said. “The light on that machine. It's painted, not photographed. Look at the edges.”

But nobody heard her, because at that moment Alex's phone started ringing. Then Olga's. Then Nina's. And outside the café window, in the building across the street, a woman was standing on her balcony and pointing at the café.

“There he is!” she shouted to the whole street. “The alien! I recognize the haircut!”

Alex touched his hair.

“What's wrong with my haircut?” he asked.

Olga took his hand.

“Alex,” she said. “I think we have a bigger problem than your haircut.”

### 2 Грамматика

### Past Perfect

Past Perfect (had + третья форма глагола) показывает, что одно действие произошло раньше другого в прошлом: Before he started his programme, he had worked as a weather forecaster. Lucy and Kennet had come too, so they watched the show. Соедините предложения, поставив более раннее действие в Past Perfect.

Пример: Grumov worked as a dentist's assistant. Then he started his programme. → Before Grumov started his programme, he had worked as a dentist's assistant.

1. Alex spoke at City Hall. Then a newspaper published his photo. → The newspaper published a photo after Alex …

2. The men from 3024 came. Then they disappeared. → The men disappeared after they …

3. Nina closed the café. Then they watched the programme. → They watched the programme after Nina …

4. Grumov collected evidence. Then he went on air. → Before Grumov went on air, he …

5. Nobody saw Grumov smile. → Nobody … ever … Grumov smile before that evening.
